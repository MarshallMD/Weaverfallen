A GUILD ADVENTURE

The Missing Children
of Weaverfallen

Featuring Friedrich the Innkeeper and Claude Bloodburne · for four 4th-level characters · 2024 rules

Overview

What Is This Adventure?

The Missing Children of Weaverfallen is a one-session guild adventure for four 4th-level characters, using the 2024 rules.

Summary

The party has been called to Weaverfallen, an aristocratic town of sorcerer families north of Baldur's Gate, to investigate the disappearance of several children in the town. The Mayor has noted each of the children have been abducted in the night, their parents waking to empty beds. Even after magical security precautions were taken, the children continue to disappear in the night. The Mayor suspects Claude Bloodburne, a scorned sorcerer defeated ages ago, but cannot explain how his powers have returned.

The party begins the adventure at rest, in Friedrich's Inn, in Sootstone, the hamlet where the working class of Weaverfallen live.

A shared nightmare, a ghostly innkeeper and a broken, glowing stone lead the party down Remnoc's Jaunt to the Temple of Remnoc, and to a final showdown with Claude Bloodburne himself.

Adventure at a Glance

  1. Part 1: The Dream Sequence and Friedrich's Inn. Each character dreams of a child Claude is torturing, and tries to save them. Then the party wakes to a ghost reaching for a floating stone.
  2. Part 2: Journey to Remnoc's Temple. A fountain, a screeching raven, Blood Goblins and the Priests of Remnoc. Piece by piece, the Bloodstone is assembled, and Friedrich is restored.
  3. Part 3: The Final Showdown. Claude Bloodburne, in Bloodburne's Boiling Pit.

Set-Up

The party has been called on behalf of the guild to investigate the missing children of Weaverfallen. The adventure begins with them already asleep in their rooms at Friedrich's Inn.

Running the Adventure

  • Setting DCs for Opening Dream. Set DCs on the fly, and don't tell players the DC; just have them roll. Most of the group should succeed with good plans, but not everyone should. Penalize a weak plan.
  • Don't rest here. At any point, if the group takes a rest, each character makes a DC 17 Wisdom saving throw. On a failure, the character has Disadvantage on attack rolls until the end of its next combat. Encourage the party not to rest.
  • Character death. If a character dies, read “Waking Up” in Part 3 before you continue.

Running on Time

The dream sequence and the final fight always run in full, and so does the denouement. If the clock gets tight, cut the two middle fights instead.

Dream timer. In each dream, after the read-aloud, roll a d4: that's how many minutes the player has to introduce themselves and lay out their plan. Never more than 4 minutes.

Cut 1: The Fountain, no fight. Keep the raven and the grab for the second piece, but no Blood Goblins. The water runs crimson until Friedrich holds the piece. Saves about 20 minutes.

Cut 2: The Temple, no fight. The priests are surprised and happy to see Friedrich. They embrace, and it seems like a warm reunion. They somberly know he needs the third piece, and they look to one of the party to remove it and give it to Friedrich. Saves about 20 minutes and takes you straight to the final fight.

When to cut (hours:minutes since the session started, for a 3½-hour session; for 3¼ hours, make every time 15 minutes earlier):

Time elapsed when the party leaves the inn Run
Up to 1:10 Everything as written
1:10 to 1:30 Cut 1
More than 1:30 Cut 1 and Cut 2

Check again when Friedrich first speaks: if more than 1:45 has passed and the temple fight is still on, use Cut 2. The final fight needs to start by 2:20 in to finish, with the denouement, by 3:30.

These times assume the full fountain takes about 35 minutes, the temple fight about 30, the transformation 5, the final fight 55 and the denouement 15.

Part 1: The Dream Sequence and Friedrich's Inn

Welcome adventurers, to the land of Weaverfallen, a wealthy and aristocratic community as old as the Sword Coast and nearly as exclusive. The town, settled by a family of sorcerers, has cultivated its population to include only the richest and most powerful sorcerer bloodlines. An invitation to live in Weaverfallen is an invitation to the highest of magic societies.

The Mayor has called you to investigate the disappearance of nearly a dozen children, all heirs to vast fortunes and highly gifted in magic.

The Mayor has but one suspect: Claude Bloodburne, a sorcerer who threatened the town over 400 years earlier, but who the town collectively dealt with and hasn't been seen since. Until now.

Each of you is currently lying in your beds, in a room at Friedrich's Inn in Weaverfallen, on the Sword Coast, north of Baldur's Gate. You are sleeping restfully, until your dream is interrupted by a peculiar scene.

You see a child being restrained. And somehow, every one of you can see it happening.

Each Scene

  1. Read the scene. Between scenes, a wash of gray clouds flashes before the next player's eyes. Claude Bloodburne is holding and torturing a young sorcerer child, and something different restrains the child in every scene.
  2. Friedrich appears as a wispy, bluish-hued specter, trying to aid the child, but he can't speak yet, and he has no physical form: nothing he tries touches the child, the restraints or Claude. He's only part of the narration.
  3. Claude gives his one line.
  4. Ask the spotlight question. The player answers it in character while describing how they free the child, and names the ability check or saving throw they're making. Roll a d4 first: that's how many minutes they have (see Running on Time).
  5. Set the DC on the fly, then have them roll.
  6. Write down the exact roll they used: a skill, a save, an attack or a spell. If they save the child, that roll is Branded, and Power Sap hits it once the party leaves the inn.

[DM, before the next scene: Did they roll against your DC? Every rescue gets a roll. On a success, give Heroic Inspiration and write down the exact roll they used: Power Sap hits it once the party leaves the inn.]

A thick haze clouds your vision. You were able to see the entire sequence play out just now, as if sharing the same dream.

And now, before you, is...

Same Method Twice

Each restraint should hold up against a method that already worked in an earlier scene, so players have to find a new one. Reusing the same ability is fine: two barbarians can both use Strength (Athletics), as long as the method is different. This is your call, with no DC change.

Turning It on Claude

Players can act against Claude directly; within reason, anything goes, and he retains his stat block (Part 3). If a player frames the rescue as an attack on Claude or an attempt to outwit him, Claude makes the roll instead: d20 + 4 against the player's spell save DC, or 8 + proficiency bonus + the relevant modifier for non-casters. If Claude fails, the player passes. A great answer gives Claude Disadvantage.

Results

  • Successful rescue. The PC gains Heroic Inspiration, and the roll they used is Branded (see Power Sap, Part 2).
  • Failed rescue. The PC fails to save the child, and Claude absorbs them. The table sees something horrific; you narrate it. A PC who simply tried something that didn't work takes no damage. If the PC's plan put their own body in harm's way, they make the scene's Danger Save. A failed rescue isn't Branded: Power Sap skips that character.
  • The stakes. Every child the party fails to save comes back as a henchman: a Priest of Remnoc in the temple fight, who rises again as a Bloodburne Priest in the final confrontation. There are never fewer than two priests: if two or more rescues fail, use the number of failures.

Danger Saves (Optional)

Use only when a failed rescue left the PC in harm's way: DC 13, 2d6 damage on a failure, half on a success.

2. Friedrich's Inn: The Main Hall

[DM: Before you read on, check that everyone who saved a child has Heroic Inspiration and that every rescue's roll is written down.]

A gray mist descends over everyone's eyes once again, and you feel the release of the dream as you awake in an inn.

You each put on your adventuring gear and head down to the main hall of the inn, where you see a strange sight:

Floating in the middle of the room is a peculiar stone, like a piece of a broken geode, pulsing with a red glow.

Below the floating stone, you see the same blue-hued, specter-like figure leaping up at it. It's maybe 12 feet up in the air, and despite the athleticism of this ghost, it cannot reach it. What would you all like to do?

The Bloodstone

The Bloodstone is a geode-like stone, broken cleanly into three pieces. The pieces are magical and pulse with a red glow. Each piece given to Friedrich restores more of him. As Friedrich tells it, he enchanted the stone himself to trap Claude, pouring his own power into it, so as he regains the pieces, his power grows and Claude's weakens. (The Truth, Part 3, explains what's really happening.)

Piece Where What it restores
1 This hall Physical form. He can interact with things, but can't talk or cast spells.
2 The fountain (area 3) His voice and his spells.
3 Remnoc's Monolith (area 4) The whole Bloodstone, and everything that comes with it (Part 3).

Getting the First Piece Down

The piece floats about 12 feet up. Let any reasonable plan work: a boost, climbing, stacking furniture, Mage Hand. A character with high Dexterity off a boost can just grab it, no roll. The story doesn't move forward until a player gives the piece to Friedrich.

Friedrich, Mute

The moment he holds the first piece, the blue figure is no longer blue. Patches of his body form into corporeal substance, spreading to life until every part of him is a fully blood-pumping human. He can stand and touch things, but he can't talk or cast spells. He points and gesticulates to get the party to investigate things. Communicating with him should be an interesting interaction that doesn't give away the whole story.

Announcement: The First Piece

The moment the stone settles into Friedrich's palm, it flares a deep crimson, and a pulse of light rolls across the main hall, rattling the tankards on their hooks. You did it.

The blue drains from Friedrich's form. Color floods into him, skin and cloth and blood, until a living man stands before you, flexing fingers he hasn't felt in a very long time. He looks at each of you in turn. His lips move, but no sound comes out.

He presses the stone to his chest, and a warmth rolls out from it and settles over each of you, like stepping in from a winter night to stand beside a roaring hearth. The heat sinks deep into your skin, and somehow you know that fire won't bite you the way it used to.

Then he raises an open hand to you, the way a host waves off a guest's bill. It feels like a promise: if you stumble, he'll be there to set it right.

Tell the table: these last for as long as Friedrich is with you.

  • Friedrich is now your ally. He's solid, and he takes his own turn in combat. He can't speak or cast spells yet. He can touch, carry and point at things, and that's how he'll communicate.
  • Hearth-Warmed. Each of you has Resistance to Fire damage. (Homebrew. Closest first-party rule: Protection from Energy, PHB 2024.)
  • On the House (3 times a day). When you fail a saving throw or miss an attack within 60 feet of him, Friedrich can let you reroll. You must use the new roll. (Homebrew. No exact first-party match.)

Part 2: Journey to Remnoc's Temple

The party sets out from Sootstone for the Temple of Remnoc. Along the way, they assemble the Bloodstone and give Friedrich back his voice.

Announcement: Power Sap

As you step out of the inn and onto the path, a chill runs through every one of you who saved a child in the dream. You feel the very skill you leaned on to save them being pulled out through your skin.

Everyone who saved a child: the exact roll you made to do it now takes a −3 penalty until further notice. Note it on your sheet.

Tell the table as soon as they step outside, before the raven:

  • A skill check: −3 to that skill only.
  • A saving throw: −3 to saves of that ability.
  • A weapon attack: −3 to attack rolls with that kind of weapon attack.
  • A spell attack: −3 to spell attack rolls.
  • A spell with a saving throw: −3 to your spell save DC.

Power Sap

Power Sap starts the moment the party leaves the inn and lasts until the Bloodstone is destroyed or the dream ends. Only characters who saved their child are sapped; a failed rescue isn't Branded. It hits only the exact roll the character used in the dream, at −3. Damage, AC, initiative, hit points and every other roll stay as they are. There's no save, it isn't a spell, and it can't be stopped.

Used in the dream Power Sap
Athletics to climb out −3 to Athletics checks. Strength attacks are untouched.
A longsword attack −3 to attack rolls with Strength weapons.
Arcana −3 to Arcana checks.
Eldritch Blast −3 to spell attack rolls. Save DC is untouched.
Hold Person (a spell with a save) −3 to spell save DC. Spell attacks are untouched.
A Dexterity saving throw −3 to Dexterity saves.

3. The Fountain

The party exits the inn, located in Sootstone, the lower-class hamlet where the servants of Weaverfallen make their home. They make their way just outside Sootstone, where the path passes a fountain. At its top sits a glowing, geode-like stone, pulsing red, and the water seems to flow at its command. A giant raven is perched beside it. Suddenly, it begins to screech.

The Raven

The raven rests perched atop the fountain and suddenly begins screeching. Any attack that lands on it scares it off. Basically any interaction scares it off. The resulting confusion is the point: a player might figure out that it's an alarm clock in the real world.

The Second Piece

The second piece sits at the top of the fountain, seemingly controlling the flow of its water. Lifting it off turns the water crimson red, which alarms Friedrich, and he motions for the party to give him the stone. Giving it to Friedrich restores the water to crystal clear.

As you lift the stone off of the fountain, the water flowing from it turns crimson red, and becomes thick and viscous. Friedrich begins jumping in excitement, trying to get the attention of whomever is holding the stone. In the confusion, three forms begin to press up through the bloody fountain, their shape becoming more defined as a casing of blood traces their figures. Finally, the liquid falls away revealing three blood goblins, gnashing their teeth, ready to strike. Roll for initiative.

Blood Goblins

Just after the piece is pulled, but before it's given to Friedrich, three Blood Goblins emerge and attack. Keep the fight short; add goblins if needed.

Short on Time: The Fountain

Cut 1 (see Running on Time). Keep the raven and the grab for the second piece, but no Blood Goblins: the water runs crimson until Friedrich holds the piece.

  • Friedrich fights as a mute, corporeal ally with a real turn on his own initiative every round.
  • If the party hasn't handed over the piece by the time combat starts, Friedrich spends his turns moving toward whoever holds it and motioning for them to give it to him.

The Bloodstone in Claude's Hand

  • Bloodstone-Bound. While Claude holds the Bloodstone, his AC is 17 and he regains 10 HP at the start of each of his turns.
  • Taking it. A creature within 5 feet of Claude can take an action to try to take the Bloodstone: a DC 16 Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) check. Claude can take it back the same way.
  • Without it. Removing the Bloodstone weakens Claude considerably: he loses Bloodstone-Bound, Legendary Resistance and his legendary actions until he gets it back.
  • Destroying it. The Bloodstone has AC 15 and 20 HP while no one holds it, or anyone holding it can smash it as an action. Claude loses all corporeal form and all magical abilities beyond those of a dream, and turns back into the dream ghost he was before: still there and trying to scare them, but a lot less spooky.

Running Claude

Every move the party came to love from Friedrich comes back inverted: his Toast becomes Vicious Mockery, On the House becomes Silvery Barbs, Mind Your Step becomes Command (Approach), his Lullaby becomes Hypnotic Pattern, Ember Burst becomes Lava Tide, and Hearth-Warmed becomes The Hearth Goes Cold. If you've given Friedrich catchphrases, have Claude repeat them word for word.

Hold each mirrored move for the moment it hurts most: Silvery Barbs on the attack that would have finished a priest, Command (Approach) on whoever is reaching for the Bloodstone, routed through Lava Tide ground, and Hypnotic Pattern the first time three PCs bunch up. After that, Silence the casters, Hold Person the hardest hitter, and set the Bloodburne Priests on whoever goes for the stone. If the table is struggling, he spends his turns on spells instead of double Fire Bolts.

Claude's Playbook

His turn. Multiattack is two Fire Bolts (+8, 2d10 Fire, about 11 damage each), or one Fire Bolt and one spell. That swap is the decision every turn: control wins this fight more than 11 damage does. He holds one Concentration spell at a time (Hold Person, Hypnotic Pattern, Silence or Suggestion) and has one reaction per round, shared by Silvery Barbs and Counterspell.

Spell Use it for When
Hypnotic Pattern (1/Day) Takes clustered characters out of the fight. Don't damage the charmed ones; damage ends it. Round 1, if three or more characters bunch up
Hold Person (2/Day) Paralyzes the heaviest hitter. A priest attacking from within 5 ft scores an automatic Critical Hit. When Hypnotic Pattern ends or fails
Command (2/Day) Drop: whoever grabbed the Bloodstone. Approach: when the shortest path runs through lava. Grovel: falls Prone, turn ends. The moment someone holds the stone

Reaction priority (one per round):

  1. Silvery Barbs on a successful grab for the Bloodstone.
  2. Silvery Barbs on a successful save against his Concentration spell.
  3. Counterspell on the first radiant spell (Guiding Bolt, Spirit Guardians, a radiant smite). Lightbane makes every radiant hit cost him a legendary action.
  4. Silvery Barbs on a hit that would kill a priest.

Don't Counterspell healing on a downed character unless you've decided that character can die.

Legendary actions (3, refreshed at the start of his turn). Spend one per round early; hold the rest for the moment someone reaches the stone.

  • Lava Tide: right after a melee character moves next to him by a pit. Pairs with Command (Approach).
  • Mockery: at the end of the turn before the big hitter's turn, so the Disadvantage lands on their next swing.
  • Cinder Step: out of melee, into a lava pit (he's immune to Fire), or behind a monolith or pillar to break line of sight for spells like Hold Person and Guiding Bolt.

Traits. Save Legendary Resistance for an effect that would end the fight, above all a cleric's Turn Undead (he's Undead). Sleep can't affect him: he's immune to Exhaustion. Bloodstone-Bound is why the stone is the fight; Command (Drop), Silvery Barbs and priests standing next to him all protect it.

A three-round script.

  1. Round 1: Hypnotic Pattern on the clump, plus a Fire Bolt on someone who passed. Legendary: Mockery before the strongest unaffected character's turn, Lava Tide when someone closes in.
  2. Round 2: If the pattern still holds two or more, Mirror Image and a Fire Bolt. If it's broken, Hold Person on the striker and a priest moves in for the crits. Silvery Barbs on the first save against his spell.
  3. Round 3 on: Command (Drop) on the stone holder; Silence on clustered casters; Counterspell the first radiant spell; Cinder Step whenever melee pins him.

If the table is struggling: skip Hold Person into priest crits and Mirror Image. Spend his turns on Vicious Mockery or Minor Illusion plus one Fire Bolt.

The Truth (DM Only)

None of this adventure actually happens. The party is in a dream from the moment the session begins until just after they defeat Claude Bloodburne.

Who Friedrich is. In life, Friedrich was a dream sorcerer, and his screwing around with people in their sleep caught up to him. He was dealt with by the town, but he managed to keep himself as a noncorporeal ghost and bought the inn as a specter. Claude Bloodburne is just Friedrich's cool alter ego, the one he uses in the dream world.

Who the party is. Weary travelers on their way back from a previous adventure to return to guild headquarters. They made the mistake of choosing the cheapest inn in the richest city. They believe the Mayor sent for them, but he didn't.

What's really happening. There are no missing children. In reality, Friedrich is feeding on the party. A character who dies in the dream dies in real life, and the table learns this at the end.

How to play Friedrich. Always keep him a sympathetic, heroic figure for the team. Helpful, right up until the reveal.

The Nightengales. They really did fight Friedrich, ages ago.
